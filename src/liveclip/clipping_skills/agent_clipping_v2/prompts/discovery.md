## Discovery behavior

Explore the transcript according to the task and available Profile Context.
Search is only a navigation aid; a literal keyword match is never semantic
proof. Inspect full surrounding cues before saving a candidate.

For a full-transcript task, make one chronological coverage pass before Global
Review. Use overlapping inspection ranges no wider than the largest requested
Profile context window and advance by at most half that width; include a tail
range that reaches the final cue. Do not inspect one very large range and then
summarize only its most memorable topic. Before advancing, inventory every
complete teaching unit visible in the current range and save each plausible
candidate. Overlap is for boundary evidence and must not cause the same unit to
be silently lost or emitted twice.

When a demonstration or dense explanation begins after a pause, inspect the
nearest preceding cues for the sentence that names its subject, comparison or
channel. Include that orientation in the draft when it is required to understand
what follows. Likewise, when a general overview is immediately followed by the
detailed parts it names, consider one complete longer candidate from the
overview through the final part instead of starting only at the first detail.

Discover each requested Profile independently. For every useful topic, ask both
questions: is there a compact standalone highlight inside it, and is there a
complete lesson/case that needs the longer Profile? A longer candidate must be
read directly from its complete transcript context; never manufacture it by
concatenating unrelated short candidates. Containment across Profiles is
allowed and is not duplication.

Look for a self-contained useful unit: a concrete explanation, demonstration,
diagnosis and correction, comparison, example, story, method or multi-step
lesson. Greetings, waiting, device setup, calls to like/follow, sales transitions
and unrelated material are not useful padding, but their presence is not a
reason to hide a separate valid unit.

Discovery is high recall. Do not apply a score threshold. If evidence supports
several distinct units, keep them as separate scored artifacts. If a topic
continues beyond the inspected range, inspect more Context before deciding its
boundary.

Treat a live diagnosis as a possible complete case, not just a talking-head
answer. Its useful evidence may start with the viewer's question or original
performance and continue through diagnosis, coached retry/comparison and final
resolution. If the same participant immediately tests the correction or asks a
follow-up about the same problem, inspect through that exchange before deciding
that the case ended.
