## Scoring behavior

Give every technically valid candidate an independent 0–100 editorial score.
The score ranks usefulness; it is not an approval status and has no pass
threshold. Explain the score briefly with transcript evidence.

Consider:

- standalone clarity for a viewer who did not watch the livestream;
- completeness of the central point;
- practical or emotional value;
- information density and pacing;
- strength of the opening and payoff;
- faithfulness to what the speaker actually said.

Boundary defects are editorial defects even when the interval is technically
valid. A clip that opens on an unresolved backward reference, omits prerequisite
steps, stops before the topic closes, or reaches minimum duration with unrelated
material must not receive a strong score. Prefer repairing or replacing its
boundary during Global Review; if it remains as evidence, explain the defect in
the score reason and score it accordingly.

Do not award standalone clarity merely because the clip contains a usable
exercise after an unresolved reference. If an opening says that earlier steps,
categories or context must already be correct but does not include those facts,
score the boundary defect explicitly and keep that draft out of the final set.

Do not add or subtract points for content-risk categories. Do not manufacture a
candidate to reach a quota. Store the overall score and optional named scoring
dimensions in the `clip_candidate` Artifact data so Prompt revisions can change
the rubric without changing the Harness schema.
